What came off, and what that cost

Each of these is your instruction applied, and one of them changed the shape of the set rather than just the words on it.

Seven ads, not eight. The first set had eight and the eighth was the offer ad. An offer ad is built around a code, and you have removed the code, so that ad has no argument left to make. Rather than fake a discount back in or pad the set with a seventh treatment of an argument that is already here, it is simply not in this round. If a promotion comes back later, that ad comes back with it.

The label is blank apart from your lockup. No purity figure, no research use line, no fine print, empty compound band. Worth knowing that this sits behind a deliberately empty setting rather than a deleted one: the pipeline falls back to a default that prints a purity claim and the research wording if that setting is merely missing, so it is held empty on purpose and a check runs on every build to prove it.

The word RESEARCH is still under AXION, and that is your call to make. It is part of your actual logo, copied from your own product photograph, so taking it out would mean inventing a lockup your brand does not use, which is the reason the very first version was rejected. It is on the vial as brand, not as a claim. The before and after are side by side at the vial page. Say the word and it comes off, and it means re-rendering the master vial and all fourteen files, which is quick.

No percentage anywhere, including the one on your own site. Your storefront publishes a purity figure and it is real, but a percentage on paid-social artwork is the kind of thing that gets a whole account looked at, so it stays off the image. The subhead does the same job a different way: purity you can check yourself.

Every button is one of the two you named. Learn More and View Coa, alternating on purpose: View COA where the ad is arguing about the paperwork, Learn More where it is arguing about the product or the brand. View COA is a real destination, your COA page is live, so it is not a button that goes nowhere.

How these were checked

Worth one paragraph, because the whole point of this round is the words that are not on the artwork, and that is not something you can confirm by glancing at a thumbnail.

The artwork was read, not just looked at. Every one of the fourteen files was put through text recognition at full size and again at double size, and the text that came back was checked against a list: research use wording, any discount or code or offer language, any percentage, any price, any dose, any compound name, any purity claim. Nothing on that list was found on any file.

The check was proved capable of failing first. A check that cannot fail is not a check. Before it was run on your ads it was run on a frame deliberately carrying "For research use only, 30% off, code SUMMER30", and it caught all three. It was also run against your own approved subhead to be sure it does not fire on wording you do want. Two hundred and twenty nine checks in total, all passing.

Six files were thrown away and re-made, and none of it was compliance. One invented a line of copy that was never in the brief. One drew the button in a shape that made it read as a fourth quiz answer. The rest were re-rolled over the accent described below. None of those is something an automated check can see, which is why every file also gets looked at by eye at full size before it goes anywhere.

One thing I have not fixed, and you should see it before these run. On three of the fourteen files the word DOCUMENTED is drawn with a small accent over the E, so it reads DOCUMENTÉD. They are the two Features files and the square Us vs Them file; the other five files carrying that word are clean, checked one at a time at high magnification. It is invisible at feed size and obvious if you zoom in. The image model adds it on its own; the instruction not to is already in the prompt and it was ignored on every re-roll, and text recognition reads the word as correct on two of the three, so no automated check catches it either. I could paint it out, and I built the tool and then threw it away: on the vertical layouts it removed the comma from EVERY LOT instead, because the comma of the line above sits in exactly the same place as an accent on the line below. I would rather hand you a typo I have told you about than artwork I quietly damaged. The reliable fix is to stop letting the model draw the headline at all and set that type in code over the render, which is how the other sets here are built, and it makes every future wording change free. Say the word and I will do that.
